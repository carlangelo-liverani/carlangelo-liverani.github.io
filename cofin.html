The long-term goal motivating our studies is to give a significant contribution to the QUANTITATIVE theory of Dynamical Systems.
More precisely, we aspire to the following. To control the motion of nearly-integrable systems for times exceeding the natural limits of physical applicability of the models. For example, after about 300 years from Newton's introduction of the equations of motion for the solar system, we can finally attempt to study its dynamics for times of the order of the life of the solar system (note that the postdiction of the past can be even more interesting than the prediction of the future, due to the connections with the evolution of climate). In addition, there exists the possibility to use such control in order to plan efficient spacecraft missions.
Extend some key results of the theory with few degrees of freedom to infinite dimensional systems. A large number of models wait the benefits of such extension: from crystals (lattices of non-linear oscillators), to PDE (Hamiltonian and dissipative), to coupled maps lattices and interacting particles systems. Beside establishing results similar to the finite dimensional case (like the development of suitable perturbation theories and suitable normal forms), one would like to approach new phenomena (like dynamical phase transitions in coupled map lattices, or metastability problems in FPU). In addition the tension toward such goals is casting new light on the finite dimensional theory creating new needs even in the latter (for example the need to develop a perturbation and an averaging theory adapted to partially hyperbolic systems).
To understand in a quantitative way the birth of the irreversibility out of deterministic microscopic systems. A typical example is the problem of the heat equation. Up to now no derivation of the Fourier law from a deterministic microscopic model (even oversimplified) is available. This means that we do not have any fundamental understanding of the problem of heat conduction. At a time when technology is reaching the mesoscopic scale, therefore testing the limits of the phenomenological description provided by PDEs, this is a really uncomfortable situation.
